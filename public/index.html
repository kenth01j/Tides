Kimud Shoal · Tabango, Leyte

About the shoal

A living sanctuary, shaped by the sea.

Kimud Shoal is presented as a marine sanctuary and aquatic and ecotourism destination in Tabango, Leyte. Its clear waters and marine life make each visit an opportunity to experience the sea with care.

A story of local stewardship

The shoal’s present conservation story is reflected in Municipal Ordinance No. 2025-07, which establishes local rules for the Kimud Shoal Marine Protected Area and Aquatic/Eco-Tourism Site. These rules prohibit disturbing thresher sharks, collecting marine life, littering, and unauthorized underwater activities.

This page focuses on the shoal’s natural setting and current conservation guidance; it does not assign an unverified founding date or historical event.

Getting here

Find your way to Kimud Shoal

Kimud Shoal is in Tabango’s municipal waters, about 13 km offshore from Barangay Tugas. Travel to Tabango by road, then arrange the boat crossing with a local licensed dive operator. Zoom and explore the map to locate the area.

Suggested approach

  1. Arrive in Leyte via Tacloban Airport or Ormoc City.
  2. Travel to Tabango by road; confirm current travel times and transport with local providers.

Marine sanctuary · Tabango, Leyte

The story of Kimud Shoal

Kimud Shoal is a marine protected area and aquatic/ecotourism site in Tabango, Leyte, known for thresher shark encounters and its underwater marine environment.

A place protected through local care

The local history documented here is the community’s ongoing work to protect the shoal. Municipal Ordinance No. 2025-07 sets the rules for the protected area and tourism site, including safeguards for thresher sharks and other marine life.

What visitors should know

Visits are managed to protect the site. Diving is limited to designated hours, 6:00 AM–2:00 PM. Fishing, collecting marine life, touching or disturbing sharks, littering, improper anchoring, and unauthorized underwater activities are prohibited.

No founding date or earlier historical event is included because it has not been verified in the available local materials.

Kimud Shoal · Dive guide

The Dive

A daylight thresher shark dive at a sunken island rising from deep water in Leyte.

The dive

The crossing is longer than the old Monad run: 45 minutes to an hour by outrigger, southeast from Bounty Beach into Leyte waters. What you drop onto is a sunken island rising from more than 200 metres of water to a flattish top at 12 to 14 metres, with cleaning stations along it at roughly 18 to 22 metres. Divers settle back from the stations and wait, facing outward, the same discipline the shoal dive has always demanded.

The threshers come up from the deep to be worked over by cleaner wrasse, and more than one animal in view at a time is not unusual. The difference from Monad is light and time: at 12 to 22 metres in daylight there is ambient light for the eye and the camera, no-stop time is generous, and the window runs from first light through the morning rather than closing at sunrise. Dive operators report their best sightings in more than twenty years.

Beyond the threshers, the shoal carries the traffic expected at the edge of a 200-metre drop: whitetip reef sharks along the reef edge, devil rays, the occasional manta, tuna, turtles, and dolphins. These are wild sharks at a cleaning station. Stay low, stay behind the group, and let the animals come in rather than swimming out to meet them.

Background

Until 2022, the thresher dive at Malapascua meant Monad Shoal, about 8 kilometres closer to the island, and Kimud was a secondary stop. In September 2022, thresher sightings at Monad dwindled over a few weeks and then stopped, and the sharks began appearing at Kimud instead.

Accounts differ on why the change happened. Evolution Diving and Alert Diver point to tiger sharks beginning to patrol Monad; Dive The World reports that threshers yielded the territory to hammerheads present from January to April. The move itself has held, and early boats now run to Kimud.

Depth, visibility, season, and access details are dive-planning estimates and can vary with conditions. Follow local operator and guide instructions.
